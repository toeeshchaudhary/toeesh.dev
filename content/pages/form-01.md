**This one isn't built. It's a plan** — a document I keep adding to, for a space I want to open, probably during college. I'm putting it up because it's the clearest thing I've written about what I think good design is actually *for*.

> FORM/01 is an affordable coffee and sandwich bar built around coworking, creativity, community, and good design.

Or, the version I actually use in my head: a place to drink coffee, eat sandwiches, work on things, make things, and stay as long as you need.

## The idea

Good coffee. Good sandwiches. Good space. Affordable enough that students and young people can actually use it regularly.

It isn't meant to be a conventional café, an expensive specialty coffee shop, or a corporate coworking space. It's somewhere you can get a genuinely good coffee for a reasonable price, grab a cheap filling sandwich, sit with a laptop for several hours — study, code, write, design, make music, read, meet people, or do absolutely nothing for a while.

The space itself is part of the product.

## You don't pay to exist

Coffee shouldn't be expensive just because the room looks good. The target is **affordable → good → consistent → accessible**, and the reaction I want is "I can actually afford to come here regularly."

So the café is deliberately built for long stays: free Wi-Fi, plenty of power outlets, comfortable seating, good lighting, air conditioning, reasonable acoustics, and tables big enough to actually work on. If someone buys one coffee and works for four hours, that's fine. **Stay as long as you need.**

## The look

The name is intentionally minimal and system-like — it should read more like a designed object or an architecture project than a café brand. The aesthetic is Swiss modernism, brutalist architecture, industrial workshop, Japanese restraint, and creative technology, in roughly that order.

Concrete, steel, wood. Information treated as design rather than decoration.

## The "01" system

The `/01` is the part I like most, because it makes the whole thing extensible:

```text
FORM/01 — COFFEE
FORM/01 — FOOD
FORM/01 — WORK
FORM/01 — MUSIC
FORM/01 — LAB
```

Products get numbered the same way — `SANDWICH / 01`, `COFFEE / 02`, `POSTER / 03`, `EXPERIMENT / 04`. The numbering makes it feel like an evolving project rather than a static business, which is the point.

## The core promise

Someone should be able to walk in with **₹200** and leave having had a good coffee, a good sandwich, a comfortable place to sit, reliable Wi-Fi, power, several hours of productive work, maybe a conversation, maybe a new idea.

And they should want to come back.

The feeling I'm aiming at is *"I could spend the whole day here"* — not because it's luxurious, but because it's comfortable, useful, affordable, beautiful, and alive.

## The long-term version

The eventual dream is bigger than a café: a large garage or industrial space, half home and half FORM/01 — bedroom, desk, computers, an electronics bench, tools and synths on one side; the café on the other.

That's a long way off. For now it's a document, and [the brand systems work](/projects) is the closest I've come to actually drawing it.
