FinStocks is a company in the finance-and-tech space, and I joined them as an **AI developer intern**. It was my first time doing this kind of work inside a real company instead of on my own — and the difference was immediate.

## Real product, real users

The biggest shift from schoolwork was simple: I wasn't building something to hand in. I was building features that went into the actual product and got used by real customers, alongside people whose full-time job was keeping that product running. Real deadlines, real feedback, real consequences if something broke.

## What it taught me

The lesson that stuck was the distance between *"it works on my screen once"* and *"it works for everyone, every day."*

A real feature has to survive messy inputs it was never designed for, fail without taking down the whole app, and stay easy for the rest of the team to maintain long after you've moved on. A clever demo isn't the finish line — a thing people can actually rely on is. That standard now shows up in everything else I build, including [my hardware](/hardware).
